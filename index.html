PREVISGEN-TB v0.1.0

Drug-resistance profiling of tuberculosis from whole-genome sequencing

An automated pipeline that turns raw M. tuberculosis sequencing reads into a quality-checked resistance report on an ordinary desktop computer, validated on 116 clinical isolates from Nepal.

Research use only. Genotypic predictions must be interpreted by a qualified clinician alongside phenotypic drug susceptibility testing. This is not a certified diagnostic device.

No Nepali isolate was phenotypically resistant to bedaquiline, clofazimine, delamanid or linezolid, so only specificity could be measured for these (100% for all four).

What the validation showed

  • Low moxifloxacin specificity has a known cause. 13 of the 15 isolates called resistant but tested susceptible carry gyrA A90V or D94A, mutations that raise the MIC only slightly and often sit just below the laboratory breakpoint.
  • A large ethA deletion is shared by 29 lineage 2.2.1 isolates. All 15 of them with an ethionamide result were resistant, and all were predicted correctly. Whether this reflects a transmitting clone is the next question to investigate.
  • Three isolates show signs of mixed infection, with several resistance mutations at intermediate frequencies. The report flags these for review rather than hiding them.
  • One discordance remains unexplained. ERR4830980 had good depth and no detected resistance mutation for a drug it tested resistant to, which may point to a mechanism outside the current catalogue.

How it works

FASTQ reads→ TBProfiler→ QC gate→ HTML report

TBProfiler 6.7.0 trims reads, aligns them to the H37Rv reference, calls variants including large deletions, and matches them to a WHO-based mutation catalogue. The QC gate then marks each sample PASS, WARN or FAIL from mapping rate, depth, lineage, coverage of key resistance genes, heteroresistance and signs of mixed infection. A failed sample never shows drug calls. Steps are orchestrated with Nextflow, each in a pinned software environment, and everything runs offline once installed.

Of the 120 isolates processed (116 Nepali plus 4 reference isolates), 113 passed QC, 7 received a warning and none failed. Benchmark: 5 isolates at 130–140× depth, 75–106 seconds each using 8 CPU threads (AMD Ryzen 7 5700X).

Limitations

  • Validation uses public data only. Prospective testing on isolates from Nepali reference laboratories has not been done.
  • The WHO catalogue was partly built from CRyPTIC isolates, so accuracy on this dataset may be optimistic.
  • Resistant isolates for amikacin and kanamycin were too few for reliable estimates.
  • Absence of a known resistance mutation does not prove susceptibility.
